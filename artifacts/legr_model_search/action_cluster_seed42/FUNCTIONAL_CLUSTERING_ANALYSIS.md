# Functional Clustering Analysis of the Winning LEGR Model

## Executive summary

The seed-42 winning LEGR graph representation shows statistically significant organization by dominant tool-action category. Under the predeclared criterion, the result is **CORROBORATED**.

The winning GPS adapter achieved a cross-validated 5-nearest-neighbor macro-F1 of **0.9462** with a bootstrap 95% confidence interval of **[0.9102, 0.9735]**. Its shuffled-label permutation p-value was **0.0010**, and its between-class versus within-class cosine-distance gap was **0.2304** with **p = 0.0010**.

The GPS representation produced stronger overall separation than both the inherited V3 graph representation and the SBERT document-embedding control. This supports the claim that LEGR's learned graph space is organized by broad graph function—not only by topology or exact DAG identity.

## Question tested

The audit tested whether graph embeddings cluster according to the most prominent type of tool action in each graph:

- `read`
- `edit`
- `orchestrate`

The labels were not generated by an LLM. They were derived deterministically from the existing Campaign-v4 tool registry:

| Registry category | Analysis label |
|---|---|
| `DATA_RETRIEVAL` | `read` |
| `STATE_MODIFICATION` | `edit` |
| `ORCHESTRATION` | `orchestrate` |

Each graph received the uniquely most frequent label among its tools. Exact ties were labeled `mixed`, retained in the exported label file, and excluded from the primary three-class statistics. This avoids introducing an arbitrary tie-breaking rule.

## Evaluated population

All Campaign-v4 15-tool CSV files were read without modification. Rows were deduplicated by canonical graph identity before embedding analysis.

| Label | Unique graphs | Primary analysis |
|---|---:|---|
| read | 232 | Included |
| edit | 223 | Included |

The orchestrate class is substantially smaller than the read and edit classes. For that reason, the audit emphasizes macro-F1, class-balanced neighborhood purity, stratified cross-validation, and stratified bootstrap intervals rather than raw accuracy.

## Representations compared

Three 256-dimensional representations were extracted from the same trained composite checkpoint:

1. **Winning GPS adapter** — the new degree-encoded directed GPS graph adapter with four local/global layers and dual-attention readout.
2. **Inherited V3 graph tower** — the V3 directed graph representation wrapped by the research model.
3. **SBERT document control** — the frozen SBERT semantic expert's encoding of serialized DAG text. This is a semantic text control, not a graph encoder.

The model was placed in evaluation mode, embeddings were L2-normalized, and graph order was held fixed. A complete second extraction pass reproduced the embeddings with a maximum absolute difference of **5.96 × 10⁻⁸**.

## Statistical interpretation

The primary evidence was calculated in the original embedding space using cosine distance. PCA and t-SNE were used only for visualization.

The predeclared corroboration rule required all of the following for the winning GPS representation:

1. Cross-validated 5-NN macro-F1 significantly above shuffled-label performance at `p < 0.05`.
2. Between-class minus within-class cosine-distance separation significantly above shuffled-label performance at `p < 0.05`.
3. The bootstrap 95% lower bound for macro-F1 above three-class chance, `1/3`.

The GPS adapter passed all three conditions:

- Macro-F1 permutation p-value: **0.0010**
- Distance-gap permutation p-value: **0.0010**
- Macro-F1 bootstrap lower bound: **0.9102**, well above **0.3333**

With 1,000 permutations, `0.0010` is the smallest reportable p-value under the finite-sample correction. It means none of the shuffled-label trials matched or exceeded the observed statistic; it should be read as `p ≤ 0.001`, not as proof of zero probability under the null.

## Architectural interpretation

The result is consistent with the winning adapter strengthening functional organization already present in the inherited representations:

- Its silhouette score is higher than V3 and SBERT.
- Its cross-validated macro-F1 is higher than V3 and SBERT.
- Its balanced neighborhood purity is higher than V3 and SBERT.
- Its cosine-distance separation is materially larger than V3 and SBERT.

The strongest improvement is in global class separation: the GPS distance gap of **0.2304** exceeds V3's **0.1888** and SBERT's **0.1619**. This is compatible with the architecture combining semantic tool-name features with degree information, directed local propagation, global relation-aware attention, and dual-attention graph pooling.

This comparison is descriptive, not a paired significance test between the three encoders. It establishes that all three spaces contain functional information and that the GPS adapter has the strongest observed values in this audit. It does not isolate which single architectural component caused the improvement.

## What the result supports

The evidence supports the limited claim:

> The winning LEGR graph embeddings are organized by the dominant read, edit, or orchestrate function of their constituent tools, and this organization is stronger in the measured diagnostics than in the inherited V3 graph and SBERT document representations.

The result does not establish that:

- functional categories emerge independently of tool names;
- the model learned a causal theory of tool behavior;
- the clustering generalizes to unseen tools or a different dataset;
- the visualization alone proves discrete clusters;
- functional clustering alone explains the model's retrieval improvement over SBERT.

Because the category labels are derived from tool identities that are also available to the encoders, strong classification is partly expected. The meaningful finding is that the complete graph-level representation preserves and strengthens this functional organization while also encoding directed graph structure.
